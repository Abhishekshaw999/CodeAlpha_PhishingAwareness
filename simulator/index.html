Awareness & response
Knowledge center
Learn the patterns behind common phishing attempts, then test your judgement with a short knowledge check.
LEARN / PRACTISE

 

  
🎭 Social engineering
Attackers manipulate people rather than relying only on technical exploits.
  • Urgency: “Act now” to bypass careful thinking.
  • Authority: impersonating a manager, bank, or IT team.
  • Fear: threats of suspension, fines, or lost access.
  • Reward: prizes, refunds, or unexpected benefits.
  • Secrecy: asking you not to verify with anyone else.

  
🔎 Sender & domain checks
Look beyond the display name. Inspect the full sender address and the actual registered domain.
  • Check for misspellings and extra words in domains.
  • Compare reply-to and return-path values.
  • Do not trust a familiar logo or display name alone.
  • Verify using a bookmark, official app, or known contact method.

  
🔗 Link & website safety
Links can conceal destinations or imitate trusted brands.
  • On desktop, inspect a link destination before opening it.
  • Beware of shortened URLs and lookalike characters.
  • HTTPS is encryption, not a trust certificate.
  • Never enter credentials after following an unexpected message link.

  
🧯 If you clicked or shared information
Respond quickly and use trusted channels.
  • Stop interacting with the message or page.
  • Change exposed passwords using the official site or app.
  • Enable MFA and revoke suspicious sessions where possible.
  • Notify your IT/security team or relevant provider.
  • Contact your bank promptly if payment information was shared.
